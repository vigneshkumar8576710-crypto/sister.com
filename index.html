Dearest Akka,

Looking back, it feels like our bond started at the most unexpected time. You came to our school when I was in the 11th grade, right after Princy Mam left. You taught us for a short period, but because you were on the second floor and we weren’t allowed to go up, we didn’t really get the chance to connect right away.

Then came the preparation for the farewell. You came in on a Sunday to help us, and by pure coincidence, we both wore blue. For the first time, you told me, "I am there for you to accompany you," and that single moment sparked the bond we share today. From then on, you poured all your love into me, I shared everything about my life with you, and you shared yours. We grew so close and started chatting constantly on Instagram.

But it felt like time didn't want us to stay close when you were shifted to the CBSE side while I stayed in Matric. I felt so heartbroken. When I told you, you promised me that you wouldn't forget me, which gave me the courage to text you every single day. You took care of me even more than my parents do. I’ll never forget July 16 at 9:20 PM—it was the first time in my life I ever talked to someone for more than 10 minutes. We spoke for 38 minutes and 48 seconds, and it made me feel so strong knowing that someone was finally there for me.

I know things haven't been easy for you. I see how much heaviness you carry—your relationship with your parents isn't easy, and though I didn't fully understand it at first and maybe took things the wrong way, I want you to know I understand now. Even when work gets stressful and people try to target you, don't worry. I am right here for you. Your brother won't ever leave you. Even when we have our little fights, whenever you need anything, I will always be there.

It has been three whole weeks since I last saw you because you take the shuttle bus now. If I ever get the chance, I will come find you just to see you. Please never feel like no one is there for you, —I am always by your side.

You once said that you don't like being at home. In my heart, I want to tell you: If you don't like being there, come to our house. I will take care of you. But then that guilty feeling hits me, making me wonder how I could ever say that to our parents or how we could make it work when our parents wouldn't allow it.

But I promise you this: when I grow up, I will take care of you just like your own brother. You told me once that you don't have any siblings—please never feel that way again. I am here for you as a brother, always.

I don't know whether you are dad's little princess or not, but you are always my Queen.

Whenever you feel like venting—like how people in Tamil say "polamburathu"—and you say, "Polambha aalu illa ma, unta vandhu polambikitruken, Sorry.", please don't ever hold back. If you don't share things, you'll just keep them bottled up inside and start overthinking everything. So don't ever think like that—share everything and let it all out. I am your brother, and I am right here to heal you.

With all my love,
Your Brother 💕
